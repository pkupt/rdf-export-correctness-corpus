# KG export-correctness corpus

Sample set for the study of **export/serialisation correctness defects** in RDF and JSON-LD processors: cases where a serialiser writes output that is not well-formed, is mis-labelled, loses information on round-trip, or lets an export-time failure pass silently.

Revision 2026-10-12. Released publicly at https://github.com/pkupt/rdf-export-correctness-corpus (the code under MIT; the paper and the corpus data under CC BY 4.0).

The four CSVs in this directory are generated by `build_corpus.py`. Edit the tables in that script and re-run it; do not hand-edit the CSVs.

## Headline counts

- 7 third-party libraries screened across 5 languages: RDFLib (Python), N3.js (JavaScript), jsonld.js (JavaScript), Apache Jena (Java), RDF4J (Java), Oxigraph (Rust), dotNetRDF (C#). Apache Jena carries no admitted record at this revision — the two candidates it supplied were read-side conformance items and sit in `withdrawn_records.csv` — so six of the seven contribute.
- 24 screened defect records; 21 after removing the three records their maintainers closed without a fix (RDF4J #5521, N3.js #659 and #165). One further record (RDFLib #3443) is carried with a flag and its admission is unsettled; excluding it as well gives 20. See §4.4 of the manuscript.
- Class spread is uneven. After the de-bias cut, C4 (round-trip) has one third-party record and C10 (export-validation) has none. The Semantica case carries both. Stated here rather than left for a reviewer to find.

## Columns

`library`, `language`, `issue`, `url`, `year`, `class`, `one_line`, `state`, `fix_evidence`, `runtime_repro`, `trigger`, `notes`.

`trigger` is the construction that produces the defective output, and it is **required wherever `runtime_repro` claims a reproduction**. It is filled in for the five records brought into a defective state on 2026-10-10. It was added after a re-test found four records (#660, #3105, #3373, #3279) that did not reproduce under a reconstructed construction — the earlier pass recorded a `reproduced` verdict but not the construction behind it. Those four carry `trigger not recorded; did not reproduce on re-test 2026-10-10` in `notes`. A related but distinct set (#166, #660, #3373, #3279) is the records whose `reproduced` verdict was carried from the earlier pass with no construction recorded at all. All of them stay in the corpus: the flag is about our record-keeping, not about their status.

`state` is the issue state returned by the GitHub API at the time of the query (open or closed), as queried on 2026-10-11. Whether the defect still reproduces in the latest release is recorded separately, in `fix_evidence` and `runtime_repro`: a row may be `closed` and still carry `still live` evidence, and those are two different facts.

## Runtime reproduction

24 records, of which **13 were executed locally** (8 Python, 5 JavaScript) and 11 are labelled `not run`. The Java, Rust and C# records were not executed because no JVM, Cargo or .NET SDK was available on the machine used; the jsonld.js #206 record predates the runnable line. `not run` rows must not be cited as reproduced.

Of the 13 executed records, 9 carry a `reproduced` verdict and 4 were executed on a version where the defect no longer triggers; the `runtime_repro` column names the version for each. Three of the nine — N3.js #660, RDFLib #3373 and #3279 — did not reproduce on the 2026-10-10 re-test and are flagged in `notes`. The re-test covered four records in all: the fourth (#3105) is drawn from the four that had been executed only on a version where the defect no longer triggers, which is why the two sets are not the same size. The paper's §9.3 and the protocol's section 8 name the two sets separately.

```bash
# RDFLib #1395 — reproduced on 6.0.0, fixed by 6.3.2
pip install rdflib==6.0.0 && python repro/rdflib_1395.py

# jsonld.js #584 — live on 9.0.0 (side probe, outside the corpus)
npm install jsonld@9.0.0 && node repro/jsonld_584.js

# the detection baseline of the paper's §9.3 (Python: rdflib==7.6.0; Node: n3@2.7.2)
pip install -r requirements.txt && npm install
python baseline/run_baseline.py
```

## Detection baseline

`baseline/BASELINE.md` records what the checks a library plausibly already has would catch. Five records could be brought into a defective state locally; four are caught by at least one candidate gate and one — RDFLib #3443, the record already flagged as a feature request — is caught by none. Two results of that run are in the paper: an independent second implementation caught nothing the writing library's own parser missed, and a fidelity check that compares against the library's own in-memory graph caught nothing at all, because the graph in memory had already lost the information. `baseline/re_test_not_reproduced.csv` lists the four records that were re-tested and did not reproduce under the reconstructed trigger; a fifth row records N3.js #578 as not attempted, since it is parse-side and was withdrawn from the corpus in this revision.

The other executed records were run in a prior revision of this corpus (2026-09-02). Those runs are recorded in the `runtime_repro` column but their scripts are not part of this release; a second person re-running the selection from scratch would have to write them. This is stated in the paper's §4.3 and in the protocol's provenance note.

## Excluded, withdrawn and maintainer-closed records

Three records are excluded because they are not defects of the written output at all, after reading the maintainer threads or the issue text: Oxigraph #1121 (the maintainer states the output is valid RDF/XML of the same graph), RDFLib #948 (re-classified by its maintainer as an enhancement, since the output is valid Turtle) and Oxigraph #1345 (the serialiser picks a shorter matching prefix, so the emitted `myOntology:props\/name` resolves to the same IRI as `myProperties:name` — a compaction difference).

Four records that had been admitted in an earlier draft are withdrawn in this revision, after re-reading each issue against the protocol's unit of analysis: N3.js #578 (the StreamParser fails when a language tag is split across a chunk boundary), Apache Jena #2766 (the `turtle` command warns about illegal characters while parsing a legal IRI), and Apache Jena #3281 and #3931 (both ask the parsers to reject surrogate escape sequences, which RDF 1.2 forbids; #3931 records that nothing defective is written). All four are read-side defects, and only defects in the written output are admitted. They are released in `withdrawn_records.csv` rather than deleted, with the reason for each.

Three records are kept out of the headline because their maintainers closed them without a fix, marked as such in the `notes` column: RDF4J #5521 (`wontfix/specification`, a reading of the specification rather than a claim that nothing was lost) and N3.js #659 and #165 (closed in October 2026, on the ground that the validation belongs elsewhere). Each closure is a statement about where the validation belongs, not about the output, so the records stay and the counts are given with and without them. N3.js #659 is narrowed to its IRI half: the maintainer's closing note states that the control-character half is valid and round-trips, which under the protocol takes that half out of scope. One further record is carried with a flag and its admission is unsettled: RDFLib #3443 (a prior reproduction report judged it a feature request). See the paper's §4.4.

## Provenance

Prior records (19) were **inherited, not re-screened**: they were verified 2026-09-02 against the earlier internal corpus notes and reproduction report (not released), and that earlier screening pass is not repeated in this revision. New records were screened 2026-10-10 from the candidate list of the same earlier pass: ten were admitted at screening and nine remain, Oxigraph #1345 having moved to `excluded_candidates.csv` in the 2026-10-11 revision.

All samples come from public issue trackers. No private or customer data is included.
